Being shown is not being clicked

The four steps of a search

Every sale from a search passes through four gates, and each one has a separate cause of failure.

  1. Impression. Amazon showed you for the query. If this is low, it is a ranking and bidding problem.
  2. Click. The shopper chose you over everything else on the page. This is decided almost entirely by your main image, title, price and star rating, because that is all they can see.
  3. Cart add. They opened your listing and were convinced enough to add it. This is the detail page: the image stack, the bullets, the A plus content, the price in context, the reviews.
  4. Purchase. They completed. Losses here are usually price, shipping speed, or a competing offer.

Search Query Performance reports all four, for every query where you got impressions, and it reports the same four for the whole market on those queries. That market column is the entire value of the report. Your click-through rate on its own means nothing. Your click-through rate against everyone else competing for the identical search means a great deal.

Build a fixed basket first

One methodological point that decides whether your analysis is real.

The queries Amazon reports change from quarter to quarter. If you compare this quarter's report against last quarter's, you are partly measuring a change in which queries got reported, not a change in your performance.

The fix is to build a fixed basket: take only the queries that appear in every quarter you want to compare, drop your own brand terms and your competitors' brand names, and run all your comparisons inside that stable set. It is less work than it sounds and it is the difference between a trend and a coincidence.

Why brand terms come out

Searches for your own brand convert extraordinarily well and will drag every average upward. Leave them in and you will conclude the account is healthy while non-branded demand quietly erodes. Measure branded and non-branded separately, always.

Reading where you lose people

With the basket built, the diagnosis is usually obvious within an hour.

Impression share low, click-through normal

You are not being shown enough. Ranking and bidding. Fix reach, not creative.

Impression share healthy, click-through well below market

The most common pattern, and the most misdiagnosed. Amazon is showing you and shoppers are scrolling past. Everything they saw before deciding was on the search card: main image, title, price, rating, review count. Nothing on your detail page can be responsible, because they never reached it.

Click-through fine, cart add well below market

They arrived and left. That is the detail page: the image stack, whether the bullets answer the question the query implied, whether the price makes sense once they see the size, whether reviews raise an objection you never respond to.

Cart add fine, purchase below market

The narrowest problem and usually the cheapest to fix. Price against alternatives, delivery promise, or a variation that is out of stock.

A gap at the click, with a much smaller gap at the checkout, tells you the loss sits on the shelf rather than in the basket. That is a very different work plan from the one most agencies default to.

What fixes what

  • Click-through: main image, title, price band, star rating and review count. In roughly that order.
  • Cart add: image stack, A plus content, size and quantity clarity, answering the objections your reviews already name.
  • Purchase: price, delivery promise, stock on the variation people actually want.
  • Impressions: bids, ranking, relevance. The only one where spending more is the answer.

Two habits worth building. First, do not change the image and the title in the same test, because you will never know which one worked. Second, use Amazon's own experiment tool where you are eligible, because it splits traffic by customer and measures sales rather than clicks, which is the thing you actually care about.

The single most expensive mistake in this area is spending months on conversion work when the shopper never got as far as the page. Check which gate is leaking before you start.
